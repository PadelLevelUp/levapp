Redesign plan — LevelUp
A navy-and-blue rebrand carried from the app icon into the product, with communication with players as the organising job. Below: the new palette and type, then three directions each for the dashboard and messaging, a re-encoded calendar, the shared component language, and a sequence to build it in.
01 — DIAGNOSIS
Counters without meaning
The dashboard shows 1, 166, 49, 3230 with equal weight. Six tiles, no hierarchy, nothing actionable. 3230 pending validations reads as a bug, not a task.
One blue block for everything
In the calendar a full class, an empty class and a finished class look identical. The information that matters — who is missing — is invisible until you open each one.
Automation disguised as chat
Eight identical chase messages in a row. The real object is a rule ("chase unanswered invites"), but it is presented as a person-by-person inbox you have to work through.
Flat, unbranded surface
Green, blue, amber and red all appear at full saturation with no assigned roles, on default type. Nothing connects the product to the LevApp mark.

The dark theme is the brand, not an inversion
The icon's navy is already the darkest surface, so dark mode is where the product looks most like itself. Rules: surfaces step up in lightness (bg → surface → raised) instead of using borders; primary shifts to #4A9BFF with dark text on it for contrast; status colours keep their hue but sit on 12% tints; no pure black and no pure white text.

07 — THE OTHER SCREENS
Jogadores
The card grid currently shows name, email and two chips — email is the least useful field on it. Replace with: level, hand, attendance over the last 8 classes, and whether they still have no account. Sorting gains "sem conta", "faltas recentes" and "sem aula esta semana", which is how you actually pick who to invite.
AS
Abel Salgado
Nível 3-
Esquerda
Sem conta
Perfil do jogador
Today it is five near-empty cards and a red "Eliminar jogador" button given the same weight as "Adicionar avaliação". Restructure to one header (name, level, hand, status), then two tabs: Progresso (evaluations over time, strengths and weaknesses) and Histórico (classes, attendance, messages). Destructive actions move into a menu. The "no account yet" invite becomes a single share button, not a dashed amber panel.
Treino
Currently a nav item with no evident home. Proposal: a library of exercises and session plans, tagged by level, that attaches to a class in one tap and appears in the players' weekly summary. This is where the player-facing side earns its keep.
Definições & player app
Settings become a two-pane list on desktop and a plain grouped list on mobile — no dropdown section picker, no floating "Guardar alterações" (save on change). The player side reuses the same system with a three-tab shell: as minhas aulas, o meu progresso, mensagens. Coaches get the full navy chrome; players get the light theme with blue accents.
08 — SEQUENCE
FASE 1
Repaint
Palette, type, buttons, chips, cards, tab bar and sidebar. No layout changes, no new features. The app stops looking green and starts looking like the icon.
FASE 2
Painel + calendário
Ship the chosen dashboard direction and the re-encoded calendar blocks. Highest visible payoff per unit of work.
FASE 3
Comunicação
Invite-from-class, templates, automation rules with their results. The chat inbox is rebuilt last, as the log of all of it.
FASE 4
Jogador & escuro
Player-facing shell, progress and training, then dark mode across both — cheap once the surface tokens exist.
